# STEM Challenge Hub – real challenge list (sources)

_Updated Nov 4, 2026 (teacher-check list expanded): links refreshed where pages moved; all 48 challenges now have full pages in the prototype (see the last section)._

48 challenges in the Figma Library/Explore pages: 3 per subject for each grade band. Each is a real, free, published activity. Titles may be shortened; summaries are our own words, checked against each source page (no copied text). Videos in the prototype are placeholders, except Make a Water Filter, which uses NASA JPL’s official Learning Space video: https://youtu.be/bqcll80EwWk. Teachers add real video links for the other challenges.

### Needs a teacher check before real use

- **Harold the Robot (K–2):** steps and materials now come from the STEM Learning summary of the CS Unplugged activity (https://www.stem.org.uk/resources/elibrary/resource/398236/programming-languages-harold-robot). The source lists no supplies, so the materials are "a classmate or adult to act as the robot" and open space.
- **Marsbound! Mission to Mars (9–12):** materials (Equipment Cards, Design Mat, Student Guide, a die) and steps now come from the JPL lesson page (https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/lesson-plan/marsbound-mission-to-the-red-planet/).
- **Error Detection (3–5):** materials now come from the CS Unplugged "Card flip magic" PDF (https://classic.csunplugged.org/documents/activities/error-detection/unplugged-04-error_detection-original.pdf): about 25 identical two-sided cards per pair. Steps are still general.
- **Sorting Networks (3–5):** the source pages are short summaries, and the full steps are in linked PDFs that weren't read. The steps are kept general.
- **Math answers worked out by us, not taken from an answer key** (a teacher should check them):
  - Crater Curiosity: the unnamed crater is about 0.912 and Aveiro about 0.936, so the unnamed crater suggests a butterfly pattern.
  - Mars Marathon: 4,219,500 cm ÷ (π × 25 cm) ≈ 53,724 wheel turns.
  - Rad Reflection: 26.4 m² ÷ (π × 1.2² ≈ 4.52 m²) ≈ 5.8 times bigger.
- **The four Hard-level questions were written by us, not taken from the source pages** (a teacher should check them). Each follows from what the activity tests, and the correct answer is listed first:
  - Melting Ice Experiment (K–2): "Which ice cube would melt the fastest?" → One in warm, flowing water.
  - Soda-Straw Rockets (3–5): "Your rocket keeps curving to one side. What should you check first?" → That the fins are straight and even.
  - Balloon Rockets (6–8): "Your rocket stops halfway along the string. Which change would most likely help it go farther?" → Blow more air into the balloon.
  - On Target (9–12): "Your marble keeps landing past the target. What should you change?" → Release the marble earlier.
- **Source subject differs from our app:**
  - Speaking in Phases is listed as Engineering.
  - Design a Robotic Insect and What Tools Would You Take to Mars? are listed as Science.
  - Build a Light Detector and Tracking Spacecraft are listed as Technology.
- **Source grade range wider than our band:** several sources (for example Melting Ice is 2–11, and Image Representation and Binary Numbers are for ages 7+). Younger students may need help.
- **Summary update:** the Melting Ice source compares warm, cool and flowing water. Salt water is an extension. Our earlier summary said "fresh vs. salt water and still vs. moving water".
- **Optional video titles** are descriptive placeholders. Teachers add the real links.
