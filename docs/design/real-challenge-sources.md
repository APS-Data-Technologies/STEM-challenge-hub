## Notes for teachers and the PM

- **Grade range outside our band:** **Solar Oven** is listed for grades 6–8 on the source, but we placed it in 9–12. Either keep it as an easier high-school activity or move it to 6–8.
- **Wide or overlapping ranges:** many sources cover several bands, for example Robotic Arm (K–8), Building with Pasta (K–8), Marsbound (3–12) and Melting Ice (2–11). We placed each one in the band where it fits best.
- **Typos on JPL pages:** the “Pi in the Sky” pages and Planetary Travel Time show grade ranges like “6-5” or “7-5”. This is a typo on JPL’s site, not ours.
- **Subject differs from the source:** the source lists What Tools Would You Take to Mars? and Design a Robotic Insect as Science, Speaking in Phases as Engineering, Build a Light Detector and Tracking Spacecraft as Technology, and Mars Sample Return Coding as Engineering. We kept our subject so each band has 3 challenges per subject.
- **Videos:** Make a Water Filter uses NASA JPL’s official video (https://youtu.be/bqcll80EwWk). Other videos are optional links the teacher adds.

## Full challenge pages in the prototype: what a teacher should check

All 48 challenges have a full page in Figma (introduction, materials, steps, safety tip, one question), written in our own words from the source page. These items were written or worked out by us and are **not** taken directly from the source:

- **Error Detection** and **Sorting Networks:** the steps are general because the full steps are in a longer PDF. Error Detection’s materials come from the CS Unplugged “Card flip magic” PDF.
- **Harold the Robot:** the source lists no supplies, so the materials are “a classmate or adult to act as the robot” and open space.
- **Math answers worked out by us, not taken from an answer key:**
  - Crater Curiosity: unnamed crater ≈ 0.912, Aveiro ≈ 0.936, so the unnamed crater suggests a butterfly pattern.
  - Mars Marathon: 4,219,500 cm ÷ (π × 25 cm) ≈ 53,724 wheel turns.
  - Rad Reflection: 26.4 m² ÷ (π × 1.2² ≈ 4.52 m²) ≈ 5.8 times bigger.
- **The four Hard-level questions were written by us** (the correct answer is shown after the arrow):
  - Melting Ice Experiment (K–2): “Which ice cube would melt the fastest?” → One in warm, flowing water.
  - Soda-Straw Rockets (3–5): “Your rocket keeps curving to one side. What should you check first?” → That the fins are straight and even.
  - Balloon Rockets (6–8): “Your rocket stops halfway along the string. Which change would most likely help it go farther?” → Blow more air into the balloon.
  - On Target (9–12): “Your marble keeps landing past the target. What should you change?” → Release the marble earlier.
- **Daily challenge questions** (one per grade band) are general science facts written by us, not taken from these sources.

Before real classroom use, a teacher should review each activity for accuracy and safety.
